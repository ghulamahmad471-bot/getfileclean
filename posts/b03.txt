desc: Does Facebook remove metadata from photos? What happens to EXIF and GPS data when you upload, what Facebook may still keep, and how to clean photos first.
---
A common question is: **does Facebook remove metadata** from the photos you upload? The answer has two parts: what other people can see, and what the platform itself receives.

## What other people see

Facebook is widely reported to strip EXIF metadata, including GPS coordinates, from the image files it shows publicly. If someone downloads a photo from your post, they generally won't find your camera's location data inside the file.

## What Facebook receives

Stripping metadata from the public copy happens after upload. The original file, with all its metadata, is sent to the platform first. Large platforms can read that information before removing it, according to their own privacy policies. If you'd rather the platform never receives your GPS location, remove it before uploading.

## Where metadata can still slip through

- **Messenger:** files sent as attachments may be handled differently from photos in posts.
- **Groups and Marketplace:** handling can differ by feature and can change over time.
- **Visible clues:** street signs, house numbers and landmarks in the photo itself aren't metadata and won't be removed.

## The safe habit

Don't rely on any platform's processing. Platforms change their systems, and you can't easily verify what's kept. Clean photos before uploading:

1. Drop photos into GetFileClean.
2. Check whether they contained GPS or device data.
3. Upload the clean copies.

Your files are processed in your browser and never uploaded to GetFileClean.

=== does-instagram-remove-metadata

desc: Does Instagram remove metadata? What happens to EXIF and GPS data in photos and videos you post, the location tags you add yourself, and how to stay safe.
---
**Does Instagram remove metadata** from the photos and videos you post? Mostly yes for public copies, but there's more to know.

## Embedded metadata vs location tags

There are two different kinds of location on Instagram:

- **Embedded metadata:** GPS coordinates inside the photo file from your camera. Instagram is widely reported to remove this from the images it serves to viewers.
- **Location tags:** places you choose to add to a post. These are public by design and are not metadata. Instagram shows them to everyone who can see the post.

## What Instagram may still receive

Like other platforms, Instagram receives your original file during upload, metadata included. What it stores and uses is covered by its privacy policy. If you don't want to hand over precise GPS data, clean files before posting.

## Stories, Reels and DMs

Different features process media differently, and these systems change over time. Don't assume every feature treats files the same way.

## Visual clues still matter

Metadata removal doesn't hide what's in the picture: house numbers, school uniforms, car number plates or a view from your window.

## Clean before you post

Use GetFileClean to remove EXIF, GPS and device data from photos and videos before uploading. It works in your phone's browser with no uploads. See also [remove metadata from iPhone photos](remove-metadata-iphone-photos.html).

When you attach a file to an email, it's sent exactly as it is. Email services don't strip **attachment metadata**, so everything inside the file reaches the recipient.

## What the recipient can see

- **Photos:** GPS location, device model, date and time.
- **Word, Excel and PowerPoint:** author, last modified by, company, editing time and template.
- **PDFs:** author, title, creating software and dates.

The email itself also contains technical headers, but those are separate from the file metadata.

## Why this matters

Emails get forwarded, archived and stored for years. An attachment you send to one person can reach many others, metadata included. For job applications, client proposals and legal documents, leftover names and comments can be embarrassing or worse.

2. Drop them into GetFileClean and check the report.
3. Download the clean copies.
4. Attach the clean files.

Because GetFileClean runs in your browser, confidential attachments never pass through another server just to be cleaned.

Profile photos on dating apps are usually processed by the app. The risk comes later, when you move to messaging, email or another app and start sending pictures directly. That's when **photo metadata** can reveal more than you planned.

- GPS coordinates of where it was taken, which may be your home.
- The exact date and time.
- Your phone model.

1. **Turn off camera location** on [iPhone](turn-off-location-iphone-camera.html) or [Android](turn-off-location-android-camera.html).

Separately from metadata, photos you use on dating profiles can be found with reverse image search. Consider using photos that don't appear on your other public social accounts.

## Trust your instincts

If someone pushes for personal details or specific photos early on, that's a warning sign. Metadata removal is one small part of staying safe online.

Cloud storage is the easiest way to share large files, but it usually shares the **original file**, with all its metadata.

## What happens when you share a link

When someone opens a shared link and downloads a file, they generally get exactly what you uploaded. For photos, that can include GPS location and device details. For documents and PDFs, it includes author names, company fields and editing history.

## Photo services are different from file storage

Photo services such as Google Photos and iCloud Photos have their own sharing settings, including options to leave out location in some cases. Plain file storage such as Drive folders and Dropbox doesn't usually change your files.

## The "set and forget" problem

Shared folders often stay open for months. Files added later inherit the same sharing, and their metadata goes along with them.

## A safer workflow

1. Create a separate "to share" folder.
2. Clean files with GetFileClean before putting them in that folder.
3. Share only that folder.
