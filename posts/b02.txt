desc: How to remove metadata from iPhone photos: turn off location when sharing, remove location in the Photos app, or strip all metadata with a free browser tool.
---
iPhone photos store a lot of metadata, including the date, device model, camera settings and often GPS location. Here are three ways to **remove metadata from iPhone photos** before you share them.

## Method 1: Turn off location in the share sheet

When you share from the Photos app, iOS lets you leave out location for that share:

1. Select the photo or photos and tap **Share**.
2. Tap **Options** at the top of the share sheet.
3. Turn off **Location**. Some iOS versions also offer **All Photos Data**. Turn that off too if you see it.
4. Tap **Done** and share as normal.

This only applies to that share. It doesn't change the original photo.

## Method 2: Remove location in the Photos app

You can remove the location from a photo you've already taken:

1. Open the photo and swipe up, or tap the info button.
2. Tap **Adjust** next to the location.
3. Choose **No Location**.

This removes GPS from that photo but leaves other metadata such as the date and device model.

## Method 3: Strip all metadata with GetFileClean

For a complete clean, including device, dates, software and location:

1. Open GetFileClean in Safari on your iPhone.
2. Tap **Choose files** and select photos from your library.
3. Review the report to see what was found.
4. Tap **Download** to save the clean copy.

iPhone photos are often saved as HEIC. If you pick them through the browser, iOS usually converts them to JPG automatically, and GetFileClean cleans them without re-compressing. Nothing is uploaded.

## Stop location being saved in the first place

If you don't want new photos to carry GPS data, see [how to turn off location for the iPhone camera](turn-off-location-iphone-camera.html).

## Which method should you use?

- Quick share to a friend: Method 1.
- Fixing one photo in your library: Method 2.
- Sending files to strangers, marketplaces or clients: Method 3, because it removes everything, not just location.

title: How to Turn Off Location on iPhone Camera (Stop Photo Geotags)

---
By default, the iPhone Camera app asks for location access, and many people allow it without thinking. Here's how to **turn off location on the iPhone camera** so new photos stop recording GPS metadata.

## Steps to turn off camera location

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Privacy & Security**.
3. Tap **Location Services**.
4. Scroll down and tap **Camera**.
5. Choose **Never**.

From now on, new photos and videos taken with the Camera app won't include GPS coordinates.

## What you lose

Your Photos app won't be able to show new photos on a map or group them by place. Some people turn location on only when travelling and off at home, which is a reasonable compromise.

## What this doesn't fix

- **Old photos:** photos taken before you changed the setting still contain their location.
- **Other metadata:** date, time, device model and camera settings are still saved.
- **Other camera apps:** third-party camera apps have their own location permission. Check each one in the same Location Services list.

## Clean photos you've already taken

To remove location and all other metadata from existing photos, use GetFileClean. Select the photos in your browser and download clean copies. It runs on your iPhone in Safari, and photos are never uploaded.

For more options, read [how to remove metadata from iPhone photos](remove-metadata-iphone-photos.html).

1. In File Explorer, select one or more photos.
2. Right-click and choose **Properties**.

Windows creates copies with "Copy" in the name. Alternatively, choose **Remove the following properties from this file** and tick the fields you want to delete.

- It doesn't always remove everything. Some XMP data and app-specific blocks can remain.
- It works best on JPG. Support for PNG, WebP and other formats is limited.

- It handles PDFs and videos poorly or not at all.

---
HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) is the default photo format on modern iPhones. It stores the same kind of metadata as JPG, including GPS location, device model and dates. Here's how to **remove metadata from HEIC** files.

## Why HEIC is trickier

HEIC is newer and less widely supported than JPG. Many tools can't edit HEIC directly, and some can't open it at all. Windows may need an extension to view HEIC files.

## Option 1: Let your iPhone convert to JPG

If you share or pick photos through a browser or certain apps, iOS often converts HEIC to JPG automatically. You can also set your camera to save JPG: **Settings > Camera > Formats > Most Compatible**. You'll still need to remove metadata from the JPG.

## Option 2: Turn off location when sharing

Use the share sheet's **Options** menu and turn off **Location**. See [remove metadata from iPhone photos](remove-metadata-iphone-photos.html).

## Option 3: Convert and clean with GetFileClean

GetFileClean converts HEIC to a clean JPG in browsers that can open HEIC, such as Safari on Mac and iPhone. The new JPG contains no hidden metadata. If your browser can't open HEIC, the tool tells you, and you can convert to JPG first with your phone's settings above.

## Will converting HEIC reduce quality?

Converting to JPG involves compression, but GetFileClean uses high quality settings, so the difference is very hard to see. If you need perfect originals, keep the HEIC for yourself and share the clean JPG.

=== remove-metadata-png
title: How to Remove Metadata from PNG Files
cat: Photo Privacy
kw: remove metadata png, png metadata, png text chunks, png exif, strip png metadata
desc: PNG files can hide text chunks, EXIF and timestamps. Learn what PNG metadata contains and how to remove metadata from PNG images without losing quality.
---
PNG files don't usually come straight from a camera, but they can still carry metadata. Here's how to **remove metadata from PNG** images.

## What metadata can a PNG contain?

PNG stores extra information in sections called chunks:

- **tEXt, zTXt and iTXt:** text fields such as Author, Software, Comment, Copyright and Description.
- **eXIf:** full EXIF data, including GPS, if the image came from a camera or phone.
- **tIME:** the last modification time.
- **XMP:** editing history from apps like Photoshop.

Some image generators and editors also write their settings or prompts into PNG text chunks.

## Does removing PNG metadata reduce quality?

No. PNG is lossless, and removing metadata chunks doesn't touch the image data. GetFileClean removes the text, EXIF, XMP and time chunks and keeps the chunks needed to display your image correctly, including transparency and colour information.

## How to strip PNG metadata

1. Open GetFileClean.
2. Drop in your PNG files.
3. Check the report for author names, software or GPS.
4. Download the clean PNGs.

Windows' built-in **Remove Properties** option often can't edit PNG files, which is why a dedicated tool helps.

## Are screenshots PNG files?

Many screenshots are PNGs. They usually carry less metadata than camera photos, but can still include software and date information. Read [do screenshots have metadata](screenshot-metadata.html).

WebP is a modern image format used across the web. Like JPG, it can store EXIF and XMP metadata. Here's how to **remove metadata from WebP** images.

## What metadata is in a WebP file?

WebP files are built from chunks. Two of them hold metadata:

- **EXIF:** device, dates, settings and possibly GPS coordinates.
- **XMP:** editing history and descriptive fields.

There's also a header that flags whether these chunks are present.

## Remove WebP metadata without losing quality

GetFileClean removes the EXIF and XMP chunks, updates the header flags and file size, and leaves the image data untouched. There's no re-compression, so quality is identical.

1. Open GetFileClean.
2. Drop in your WebP images.
3. Download the clean copies individually or as a ZIP.

## Why clean WebP files?

WebP is common on websites and online stores. If you convert phone photos to WebP for your site, the metadata can come along. Cleaning images before publishing keeps location and device details off your pages and makes files slightly smaller.

GIFs carry less metadata than photos, but they're not always clean. Here's how to **remove metadata from GIF** files.

## What metadata can a GIF contain?

- **Comment extensions:** free text added by the software that made the GIF.
- **Application extensions:** blocks added by apps, including XMP metadata from Adobe software.
- **Looping information:** an application block that tells the GIF to repeat. This isn't personal data and is needed for animations.

## Remove GIF metadata safely

GetFileClean removes comments and app data blocks, including XMP, but keeps the looping block so animated GIFs still play and repeat correctly. Frames and colours aren't changed.

1. Drop your GIFs into GetFileClean.
2. Check the report.
3. Download the clean files.

## Why bother?

Comments and XMP can contain software names, user names or file paths from the computer that created the GIF. If you make GIFs for clients or publish them widely, a quick clean is a tidy habit.
