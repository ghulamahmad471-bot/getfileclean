GetFileClean is a free **metadata remover** that works in your browser. Here's how to use it.

## Step 1: Add your files

Open GetFileClean and either drag files onto the drop zone or click **Choose files**. You can add many files at once and mix types: photos, Word and Excel files, PDFs, videos, audio and ZIP archives.

## Step 2: Read the report

Each file appears in a list with a short report of what was found:

- **Red GPS tag:** the file contained a location, shown as coordinates.
- **Names:** author, last edited by, artist or company.
- **Device:** phone or camera make and model, serial number.
- **Dates:** when the photo, video or document was created.
- **Types of metadata removed:** EXIF, XMP, IPTC, document properties, tags.
- **Warnings:** for example, when a PDF's digital signature will no longer be valid.

A green **Clean** tag means the clean copy is ready.

## Step 3: Download

- Click **Download** next to any file to save its clean copy.
- Click **Download all (.zip)** to save every clean file in one ZIP.

Clean files get "-clean" added to their name, so you can tell them apart from your originals. Your original files are never changed.

## The counters

At the top of the list you'll see how many files were cleaned, how many had a GPS location and how many had names or device information.

## Start again

Click **Clear** to empty the list. Because files are processed in your browser, closing the page removes everything.

=== getfileclean-supported-formats

---
Here's every file type GetFileClean supports and what it removes from each.

## Images

- **JPG / JPEG:** EXIF (including GPS, device, dates, serial numbers and thumbnails), XMP, IPTC, comments, app data and extra embedded images. No re-compression. Photos with a rotation tag are turned upright first.
- **PNG:** text chunks (author, software, comments), EXIF, XMP and timestamps. Lossless.
- **WebP:** EXIF and XMP chunks. Lossless.
- **GIF:** comments and app data including XMP. Animation looping is kept.
- **HEIC, HEIF, AVIF, BMP, TIFF:** converted to a clean JPG or PNG in browsers that can open them.

## Documents

- **DOCX, XLSX, PPTX:** core, app and custom properties (author, last modified by, company, manager, title, template, dates) and metadata in embedded images.
- **ODT, ODS, ODP:** document metadata (meta.xml) and metadata in embedded images.

## PDF

Document information (author, title, creator, producer, dates, keywords), XMP metadata and private app data. Saved as a single clean version. Signed PDFs show a warning.

## Video

- **MP4, MOV, M4V, 3GP:** metadata boxes (location, make, model, software, titles), XMP and creation dates. No re-encoding, same file size.

## Audio

- **MP3:** ID3v1, ID3v2 and APE tags.
- **M4A:** metadata boxes and dates.
- **FLAC:** Vorbis comments, pictures and app blocks.
- **WAV:** INFO, bext, iXML, ID3 and XMP chunks.

## Archives

- **ZIP:** every supported file inside is cleaned. Unsupported files are kept as they are.

## Not supported yet

Password-protected files, 7z and RAR archives, and older Office formats (DOC, XLS, PPT). Convert older Office files to the newer formats first.

=== online-metadata-removers-compared
title: Online Metadata Removers Compared: What to Look For
cat: Privacy & Security
kw: best metadata remover, online metadata remover comparison, free metadata remover, exif remover online, metadata removal tools
desc: How to choose the best metadata remover: compare upload vs browser-based tools, supported formats, limits, quality and privacy before you pick one.
---
There are many **metadata removers** online. Before choosing one, compare them on the points that actually matter.

## 1. Does it upload your files?

This is the most important question. Upload-based tools send your file to a server. Browser-based tools process files on your device. If you're cleaning files for privacy, uploading them somewhere else works against you.

## 2. Which formats does it support?

Many tools handle only JPG photos. Look for support for the files you actually share: PDFs, Word and Excel files, videos, audio and archives.

## 3. Does it remove everything?

Some tools remove EXIF but leave XMP and IPTC, or clear PDF properties but leave the XMP copy. A good tool removes all metadata types and tells you what it found.

## 4. Does it keep quality?

Tools that re-save images can reduce quality. Look for lossless removal for JPG, PNG and video.

## 5. What are the limits?

Common limits include maximum file size, number of files per day, and paid tiers for batches. Browser-based tools don't have server costs, so they rarely need limits.

## 6. Does it handle rotation and colour?

Look for tools that keep photos upright and keep the colour profile.

## 7. Does it show a report?

Seeing what was found, such as GPS coordinates or author names, helps you understand your risk and confirms the tool did its job.

## How GetFileClean measures up

GetFileClean is browser-based, supports photos, Office files, PDFs, video, audio and ZIP, removes EXIF, XMP, IPTC and document properties, keeps quality and colour, keeps photos upright, shows a report for every file and has no limits.

=== ai-images-metadata-content-credentials
title: AI-Generated Images, Metadata and Content Credentials
cat: Technical
kw: ai image metadata, content credentials c2pa, ai generated image metadata, remove ai metadata, c2pa metadata
desc: AI image tools can add metadata and Content Credentials (C2PA) to images. Learn what they contain, how metadata removal affects them, and responsible use.
---
Many AI image generators and editing tools add information to the images they produce. Some of it is ordinary metadata; some is newer **Content Credentials** based on the C2PA standard.

## What AI image files can contain

- **Software tags** naming the generator or editor
- **Prompts and settings**, written into PNG text chunks or XMP by some tools
- **Content Credentials (C2PA):** signed provenance information describing how the image was made and edited
- **Invisible watermarks** embedded in the pixels by some providers

## Privacy considerations

Prompts stored in images can reveal more than you expect, such as personal details, client names or project ideas typed into a prompt. If you share AI images for work, check what text is embedded.

## How metadata removal affects provenance

Metadata removers delete metadata-based provenance such as XMP, text chunks and, in JPEG files, embedded credential data. Invisible watermarks are part of the image pixels, so metadata removal doesn't affect them.

## Be honest about AI content

Removing metadata to hide that an image is AI-generated, in order to mislead people, is not okay, and some platforms and laws require AI content to be labelled. If you remove metadata for privacy, label AI images clearly where it matters, in captions, posts or descriptions.

## When cleaning makes sense

- Removing personal prompts before sharing
- Stripping software details from client work, with your client's knowledge
- Keeping file sizes small for websites, with AI use disclosed elsewhere

=== screenshot-metadata
title: Do Screenshots Have Metadata?
cat: Photo Privacy
kw: screenshot metadata, do screenshots have exif, screenshot location data, screenshot privacy, iphone screenshot metadata
desc: Do screenshots have metadata? What screenshots on iPhone, Android, Windows and Mac usually store, why they rarely include GPS, and what to check before sharing.
---
Screenshots feel safer than photos. Do **screenshots have metadata**? Usually a little, rarely location.

## What screenshots usually contain

- **Date and time** taken
- **Software or device** information, depending on the system
- **Image dimensions** and sometimes a description, such as "Screenshot"
- **Colour profile**

Screenshots usually don't include GPS coordinates because they don't come from the camera, though behaviour varies by device and app.

## The bigger risk: what's in the picture

Screenshots capture everything on screen:

- Notifications, usernames and contact names
- Battery, time, network name and carrier
- Browser tabs, bookmarks and open apps
- Email addresses and phone numbers
- Parts of conversations you didn't mean to share

Crop and blur carefully, and remember the cropped part may still be there in some editing workflows. See [hidden thumbnails in JPEG files](hidden-thumbnails-in-jpeg.html).

## Screenshots of photos

A screenshot of a photo doesn't contain the original photo's GPS data. It's a new image. That's a quick trick for sharing a single image without its location, though quality is reduced and visible details remain.

## Cleaning screenshots

GetFileClean removes text tags, EXIF and timestamps from PNG and JPG screenshots without reducing quality.

=== metadata-glossary
title: Metadata Glossary: 40 Terms Explained Simply
cat: Metadata Basics
kw: metadata glossary, metadata terms, exif terms, metadata definitions, file metadata vocabulary
desc: A plain-English metadata glossary: EXIF, XMP, IPTC, GPS IFD, ID3, ICC, C2PA, document properties and more, all explained simply.
---
A quick reference to the terms you'll see when dealing with file **metadata**.

## General

- **Metadata:** information about a file, stored inside it or alongside it.
- **Hidden data:** content in a file that isn't visible by default, including metadata, comments and hidden text.
- **Lossless removal:** removing metadata without changing the main content or quality.
- **Re-encoding:** saving media again with compression, which can reduce quality.

## Images

- **EXIF:** camera-generated metadata in photos. See [what is EXIF data](what-is-exif-data.html).
- **GPS IFD:** the EXIF section that stores location.
- **Maker notes:** manufacturer-specific EXIF data.
- **Orientation tag:** tells viewers how to rotate an image.
- **EXIF thumbnail:** a small preview image stored in EXIF.
- **IPTC:** descriptive metadata such as captions, keywords and copyright.
- **XMP:** Adobe's flexible XML metadata format.
- **ICC profile:** describes an image's colour space.
- **APP segments:** sections of a JPEG file that hold metadata.
- **PNG chunks:** sections of a PNG file, such as tEXt and eXIf.
- **MPF:** Multi-Picture Format, extra images stored in one JPEG.
- **HEIC:** Apple's default photo format.
- **C2PA / Content Credentials:** signed provenance information about how media was made.

## Documents

- **Document properties:** author, title, company and dates in Office files.
- **Core properties:** standard properties in Office files (core.xml).
- **Extended properties:** application, company and manager (app.xml).
- **Custom properties:** organisation-specific fields.
- **Document Inspector:** Microsoft Office's hidden data tool.
- **Tracked changes:** recorded edits in a document.
- **meta.xml:** metadata section in OpenDocument files.

## PDF

- **Info dictionary:** classic PDF properties.
- **Creator:** the app that made the original document.
- **Producer:** the software that made the PDF.
- **Incremental update:** edits added to the end of a PDF file.
- **Redaction:** permanently removing visible content.
- **Sanitise:** removing all hidden information.

## Video and audio

- **Atoms / boxes:** sections of MP4 and MOV files.
- **udta / meta boxes:** where video metadata is stored.
- **ID3:** MP3 metadata tags.
- **Vorbis comments:** FLAC and Ogg metadata.
- **INFO chunk:** WAV metadata.
- **BWF / bext:** broadcast metadata in WAV files.
- **iXML:** production metadata from field recorders.

## Files and privacy

- **File system dates:** created and modified dates stored by your computer, not in the file.
- **Data minimisation:** sharing only the personal data needed.
- **OSINT:** open-source intelligence, finding information from public sources.
- **Provenance:** the history of where a file came from.
- **Browser-based processing:** handling files on your device rather than on a server.
