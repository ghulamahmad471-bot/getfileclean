title: Metadata and Source Protection: A Guide for Journalists
cat: Professionals
kw: journalist metadata, source protection metadata, leaked document metadata, protect sources files, newsroom metadata
desc: Metadata can expose journalistic sources through documents, photos and recordings. A practical guide to removing metadata and protecting sources.
---
For journalists, **metadata** isn't an abstract privacy concern. Documents, photos and recordings published or shared carelessly can point back to the people who provided them.

## How metadata can expose a source

- **Document properties:** an author or "last modified by" name inside a leaked file.
- **Printer and scanner details:** device names and dates in scanned PDFs.
- **Photo location and device:** a photo's GPS coordinates or the phone model that took it.
- **Timestamps:** creation dates that narrow down who had access and when.
- **Embedded images:** photos inside documents that keep their own location data.

## Metadata is only one layer

Visible features can identify sources too, such as unique wording, formatting, and tracking marks some printers add to pages. Publishing an original document as-is, even if cleaned, carries risk. Many newsrooms retype, describe or recreate documents instead of publishing originals.

## A safer handling workflow

1. Keep originals untouched and secure for verification.
2. Work on copies.
3. Remove metadata from any file that leaves the newsroom or is published.
4. Check images and documents for visible identifying details.
5. Consider publishing reconstructions instead of originals.

## Why local processing matters

Uploading sensitive files to an online tool means trusting a third party. GetFileClean processes everything in your browser. Files never leave your computer, and the tool keeps working with the network disconnected.

Legal documents go through many drafts and hands. Their **metadata** and hidden content can reveal strategy, earlier positions and confidential client details.

## Common risks in legal documents

- **Tracked changes** showing earlier offers or wording
- **Comments** with internal advice
- **Author and company** fields showing a document was reused from another client's matter
- **Template names** and document IDs from document management systems

## Professional duties

Lawyers generally have duties of confidentiality and competence that extend to electronic documents. Many bar associations have issued guidance on metadata. Equally, litigation can require preserving metadata. Follow your jurisdiction's rules and your firm's policies. This article is general information, not legal advice.

1. Finish the document and resolve all tracked changes and comments.
2. Run Word's Document Inspector.
3. Export to PDF if appropriate, and redact properly if needed.
4. Remove remaining metadata with GetFileClean.
5. Do a final check of properties and content before sending.

## Why browser-based matters for law firms

Client documents shouldn't be uploaded to third-party websites for cleaning. GetFileClean processes files entirely in the browser. Nothing is transmitted, which is easier to square with confidentiality obligations.

=== students-assignments-metadata
title: Students: What Metadata Is in Your Assignments?
cat: Professionals
kw: student assignment metadata, essay file metadata, coursework document properties, word metadata students, assignment author name
desc: Assignment files carry metadata such as author names, editing time and dates. Learn what's in your coursework files and how to keep them tidy.
---
Every essay and assignment file you submit carries **metadata**. Most of the time it's harmless, but it's worth knowing what's there.

## What's in an assignment file?

- **Author:** the name on the computer or Office account used to create the file.
- **Last modified by:** the last person who saved it.
- **Total editing time** and revision number in Word documents.
- **Created and modified dates.**
- **Template** names.

## Shared and borrowed computers

If you use a family computer, a library PC or a friend's laptop, the author field may show someone else's name. That can be confusing for whoever marks your work.

## Group projects

Group documents show who edited last and, through tracked changes and comments, who wrote what. Clean up comments and changes before final submission.

## Academic integrity

Don't try to use metadata changes to misrepresent when or how work was done. Institutions may look at a range of evidence, and misrepresentation is a serious integrity issue. Cleaning metadata is about privacy and tidiness, not hiding the truth.

## When cleaning makes sense

- Sharing your work publicly or in a portfolio.
- Submitting to anonymous review where names shouldn't appear.
- Sending documents from a shared computer.

Check your institution's submission rules first. Some ask for specific formats or properties.

=== photographers-copyright-metadata
title: Should Photographers Remove Metadata? Keep Copyright, Remove Location
cat: Professionals
kw: photographer metadata, copyright metadata photos, iptc copyright, remove gps keep copyright, photographer exif privacy
desc: Photographers rely on copyright metadata but may not want to share GPS and gear details. Learn what to keep, what to remove and how to do both.
---
Photographers have a more complicated relationship with **metadata** than most. Some of it protects your work. Some of it exposes your locations and equipment.

## Metadata photographers often want to keep

- **Copyright notice**
- **Creator name** and contact details
- **Usage terms** and licensing information

These IPTC and XMP fields help clients and publishers credit you and know how an image may be used.

## Metadata you may want to remove

- **GPS location:** client homes, private shoots and your own studio or home.
- **Camera and lens serial numbers:** they link all your images together and can identify stolen gear, but you may not want them public.
- **Editing software details and history.**
- **Exact capture times.**

## How to keep some and remove the rest

Most editing software can choose which metadata to include on export, such as "copyright only" or "all except camera and location info". Use those export settings for selective control.

GetFileClean is built for complete removal. It strips everything, including copyright fields. Use it when you want a fully clean file, such as images for personal sharing, social posts, or files where your client will add their own metadata.

## Visible protection

Metadata is easy to remove by anyone, so it isn't strong protection on its own. Visible watermarks and registration where available offer more protection for your copyright.

Proposals and tender responses are usually built from earlier ones. That's efficient, but **metadata** can reveal the reuse.

## What bid documents can reveal

- **Title property** still naming a previous client or project
- **Author and company** fields showing a partner or subcontractor wrote sections
- **Creation dates** years before the current tender
- **Template** names
- **Comments and tracked changes** with pricing discussions

## Why it matters

Evaluators want to see a response written for their requirements. A properties field naming a competitor's client undermines that impression, even if the content is tailored.

## A pre-submission checklist

1. Accept or reject all tracked changes and delete comments.
2. Search for previous client names in the visible text.
3. Check headers, footers and hidden text.
4. Export PDFs if the submission allows.
5. Clean all files with GetFileClean.
6. Open the final files and check their properties.

## Large submissions

Tenders often require many files. GetFileClean cleans them all at once and lets you download a single ZIP, without uploading confidential pricing to an outside server.
