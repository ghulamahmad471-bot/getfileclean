Protecting Children's Privacy: Remove Metadata from Kids' Photos

Privacy & Security · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

Parents share thousands of photos of their children with family, schools, clubs and group chats. Many of those photos carry location metadata that points to where a child lives, learns and plays.

What photo metadata can reveal about a child

  • The family home, through GPS coordinates.
  • The school or nursery, from photos taken at drop-off or events.
  • Daily routines, from timestamps over weeks and months.

Where kids' photos leak

  • Photos sent as files to group chats or class groups.
  • Emails to relatives or teachers.
  • Shared cloud albums and links.
  • Photo uploads to club, school or community websites.

Simple protective steps

  1. Turn off camera location for everyday photos. See guides for iPhone and Android.
  2. Clean before sharing outside your close family. GetFileClean removes location, device and date data in seconds.
  3. Check what's visible: school logos, uniforms, name tags and house numbers.
  4. Ask before others share: let relatives know you'd prefer photos of your children not to be posted publicly.
