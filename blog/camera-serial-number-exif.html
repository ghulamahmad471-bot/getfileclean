Camera Serial Numbers in EXIF: How Photos Link Back to You

Technical · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

Many cameras, especially DSLRs and mirrorless cameras, write their serial number into the EXIF data of every photo.

Where serial numbers are stored

  • Body serial number: a standard EXIF tag for the camera body.
  • Lens serial number: a standard tag for the lens.
  • Maker notes: a manufacturer-specific EXIF section that can hold serial numbers and other internal data.

Why it matters

A serial number is a unique identifier. If photos you post under different names or accounts all carry the same serial number, they can be linked to the same camera, and possibly to you if any of those photos are connected to your identity.

Legitimate uses

Serial numbers aren't all bad. Some services use them to help photographers find stolen cameras by searching for images taken with that serial number. Photographers may also use them to prove which images came from their gear.

Phones are different

Phone photos don't usually include a unique serial number in standard EXIF fields, but they include the device model, software version and other details.
