How to Create a Metadata Policy for Your Team

GetFileClean TeamEcommerce & Business · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

A metadata policy doesn't need to be long. It needs to be clear enough that people follow it. Here's a simple structure.

1. Purpose

One or two sentences: "To prevent personal and confidential information hidden in files from being shared unintentionally."

2. When files must be cleaned

  • Files sent to anyone outside the organisation
  • Files published on websites or social media
  • Files submitted to tenders, courts or regulators, unless rules require original metadata
  • Photos taken on location

3. What to check

  • Document properties: author, company, title, template
  • Tracked changes, comments and hidden text
  • Photo location and device data
  • PDF properties and proper redaction

4. Exceptions

  • Records that must be preserved with original metadata
  • Digitally signed documents
  • Files where copyright or credit metadata is required

5. Tools

Name the approved tools. For example: Word's Document Inspector for comments and tracked changes, and GetFileClean for removing metadata from all file types without uploading them.

6. Responsibility

The person sending or publishing a file is responsible for cleaning it. Managers check high-risk documents.

7. Training

Include a ten-minute walkthrough in onboarding. Show one real example of metadata in a file. It's more convincing than any rule.

Keep it practical

The best policy is one people actually follow. A fast, free, no-install tool removes the main reason people skip the step.
