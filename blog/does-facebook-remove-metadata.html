Does Facebook Remove Photo Metadata? What You Should Know

Sharing & Social Media · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

A common question is: does Facebook remove metadata from the photos you upload? The answer has two parts: what other people can see, and what the platform itself receives.

What other people see

Facebook is widely reported to strip EXIF metadata, including GPS coordinates, from the image files it shows publicly. If someone downloads a photo from your post, they generally won't find your camera's location data inside the file.

What Facebook receives

Stripping metadata from the public copy happens after upload. The original file, with all its metadata, is sent to the platform first. Large platforms can read that information before removing it, according to their own privacy policies. If you'd rather the platform never receives your GPS location, remove it before uploading.

Where metadata can still slip through

  • Messenger: files sent as attachments may be handled differently from photos in posts.
  • Groups and Marketplace: handling can differ by feature and can change over time.
  • Visible clues: street signs, house numbers and landmarks in the photo itself aren't metadata and won't be removed.
