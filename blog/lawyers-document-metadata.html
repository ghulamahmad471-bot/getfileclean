Metadata for Lawyers: Protecting Client Documents

Professionals · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

Legal documents go through many drafts and hands. Their metadata and hidden content can reveal strategy, earlier positions and confidential client details.

Common risks in legal documents

  • Tracked changes showing earlier offers or wording
  • Comments with internal advice
  • Author and company fields showing a document was reused from another client's matter
  • Template names and document IDs from document management systems
  • PDF properties carried over from the Word draft

Professional duties

Lawyers generally have duties of confidentiality and competence that extend to electronic documents. Many bar associations have issued guidance on metadata. Equally, litigation can require preserving metadata. Follow your jurisdiction's rules and your firm's policies. This article is general information, not legal advice.

A practical workflow

  1. Finish the document and resolve all tracked changes and comments.
  2. Run Word's Document Inspector.
  3. Export to PDF if appropriate, and redact properly if needed.
  4. Remove remaining metadata with GetFileClean.
  5. Do a final check of properties and content before sending.

Why browser-based matters for law firms

Client documents shouldn't be uploaded to third-party websites for cleaning. GetFileClean processes files entirely in the browser. Nothing is transmitted, which is easier to square with confidentiality obligations.
