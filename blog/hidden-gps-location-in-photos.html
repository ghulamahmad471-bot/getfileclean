Photo Location Metadata: How Your Pictures Can Reveal Your Address

GetFileClean TeamPhoto Privacy · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

Most smartphones can save the exact place where every photo was taken. This photo location metadata is stored as GPS coordinates inside the image file, accurate enough to find a specific house.

How GPS metadata gets into your photos

When location access is turned on for your camera app, the phone writes latitude, longitude and often altitude into the photo's EXIF data. The same happens with many videos. It's convenient for browsing a map of your memories, but the coordinates stay inside the file when it's shared.

Who can see your photo GPS data?

Anyone who gets the original file. Reading location metadata needs no special skill: many free apps and websites display a map pin as soon as a photo is opened. Common ways the original file gets shared include:

  • Emailing photos as attachments
  • Sending photos as "files" or "documents" in messaging apps
  • Sharing cloud storage folders or links
  • Uploading to websites, forums and marketplaces that keep original files
  • Giving files to someone on a USB drive

Everyday situations where it matters

  • Selling furniture or a car online with photos taken in your driveway
  • Sharing pictures of your children at home or school
  • Posting photos of a new purchase or a delivery at your door
  • Sending photos to someone you've just met online

How to remove location from photos

Stop it at the source: turn off location for your camera on iPhone or Android.
