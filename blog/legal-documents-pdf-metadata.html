Filing Legal Documents? Check PDF Metadata First

PDF · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

Legal documents pass between parties, courts and clients, and their metadata can reveal drafting history, authors and software. A simple check before filing or sending avoids surprises.

What legal PDFs can reveal

  • Author and title from the original Word draft, sometimes naming a different matter or client.
  • Creator and producer software.
  • Dates showing when drafting really began.
  • XMP history in some workflows.
  • Comments and markup left in the PDF.

Follow the rules first

Courts and regulators have their own rules about electronic filings, and some disclosure obligations require keeping metadata. Never remove metadata from documents where the law or a court order requires it to be preserved. This article is general information, not legal advice.

A pre-filing routine

  1. In Word, accept or reject all tracked changes and delete comments.
  2. Run Word's Document Inspector.
  3. Export to PDF.
  4. Apply any redactions properly with a redaction tool.
  5. Clean the final PDF's metadata with GetFileClean.
  6. Open the result and check properties, text selection and every page.
