Podcast Audio Files: Metadata to Remove and Metadata to Keep

Video & Audio · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

Podcasters deal with two kinds of audio metadata: tags you want listeners to see, and hidden details you don't want to share.

Metadata worth keeping in published episodes

  • Episode title
  • Show name as artist or album
  • Cover art
  • Episode number and year

Most podcast hosts set these from your feed settings, but tags in the file help when people download episodes.

Metadata to remove

  • Encoder and software details from your editing setup
  • Comments left during editing
  • Names of guests' recording devices or apps
  • Private frames from production tools
  • Raw recording dates on unedited guest files

Draft files are the bigger risk

The files most likely to leak private details are drafts and raw recordings shared with editors, guests and collaborators. Raw files from field recorders and apps can include device information, dates and even location.

Practical workflow

  1. Clean raw guest and interview files before sending them to editors.
  2. Clean draft episodes shared for review.
  3. For the final release, clean the file, then add only the tags you want with your publishing tool or tag editor.
