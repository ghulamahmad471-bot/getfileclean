Protest and Event Photos: Remove Metadata to Protect People

Privacy & Security · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

Photos and videos from protests, rallies and community events can carry metadata that identifies the person who took them and where they were.

What event photos can reveal

  • Exact location and time, placing the photographer at a specific spot.
  • Device model and serial numbers, linking photos to the same camera.
  • A series of locations, showing someone's movements before and after.

Protect yourself

  1. Turn off camera location before the event. See iPhone and Android.
  2. Clean photos and videos before sharing them anywhere.
  3. Share clean files instead of originals with journalists or organisers.

Protect others

Faces in photos can identify people who didn't consent. Consider blurring faces of bystanders, particularly anyone who might face risks for attending. Metadata removal doesn't blur faces. That's a separate editing step.
