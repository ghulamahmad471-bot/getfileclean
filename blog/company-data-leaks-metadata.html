How Metadata Causes Company Data Leaks (and How to Prevent Them)

Ecommerce & Business · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

Some of the most avoidable information leaks happen through metadata and hidden document content rather than hacking.

Common leak patterns

1. Tracked changes in published documents. Reports, contracts and statements published with revision history intact, showing earlier wording.

2. Author and company properties. Documents claiming to be independent but carrying an author or company name pointing elsewhere.

3. Fake redaction. PDFs where sensitive text was covered with black boxes but not actually removed.

4. Photo location. Images posted online that reveal sensitive sites through GPS data.

5. Reused templates. Proposals or letters with properties naming another client.

These patterns have appeared in public cases involving governments, law firms and companies. They're simple mistakes with outsized consequences.

Prevention

  • Policy: decide when files must be cleaned, for example anything going outside the company.
  • Training: a short session on metadata, track changes and proper redaction.
  • Tools: give staff an easy tool that works without uploading files to third parties.
  • Checks: include metadata in review steps for public documents.
