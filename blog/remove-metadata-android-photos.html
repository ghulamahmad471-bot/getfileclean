How to Remove Metadata from Android Photos

Photo Privacy · 2 min read · Updated 2026-11-25

Android phones save EXIF metadata in every photo, often including GPS location. Here's how to remove metadata from Android photos before you share them.

Option 1: Remove location in Google Photos

Google Photos lets you remove or edit an estimated location on a photo:

  1. Open the photo and swipe up to see details.
  2. Tap the edit icon next to the location if it's shown.
  3. Choose to remove the location.

Menus vary by version, and this may not remove every metadata field. It's best for quick fixes.

Option 2: Hide location in shared links

Google Photos has a sharing setting to remove location from items you share by link. Look for it in Google Photos settings under sharing options. This affects link sharing, not files you send in other ways.

Option 3: Strip all metadata with a browser tool

For a full clean, including device, dates and software:

  1. Open GetFileClean in Chrome on your phone.
  2. Tap Choose files and pick your photos.
  3. Check the report. GPS location is highlighted.
  4. Tap Download for each file, or Download all for a ZIP.

Prevent new photos from storing location

Turn off location tagging in your camera app. See how to turn off location on the Android camera.

Why bother on Android?

Android makes it easy to share original files through Gmail, Google Drive, Bluetooth and messaging apps. Many of those keep the full metadata, so a quick clean is worth it whenever a photo leaves your circle.
